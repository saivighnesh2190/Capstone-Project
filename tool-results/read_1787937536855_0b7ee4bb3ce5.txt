    56→#### Requirements defined
    57→
    58→**Functional requirements:**
    59→
    60→- The system shall accept hierarchical parent-child workload definitions.
    61→- The system shall represent CPU, GPU, memory and worker requirements.
    62→- The system shall model placement-group bundles.
    63→- The system shall support FIFO and greedy scheduling policies.
    64→- The system shall detect pending and unresolved trials.
    65→- The system shall construct an observer-only wait-for graph.
    66→- The system shall identify resource-dependency cycles.
    67→- The system shall record reserved, useful and stranded resources.
    68→- The system shall produce repeatable experiment evidence.
    69→- The system shall execute each experiment within a bounded time horizon.

    71→**Non-functional requirements:**
    72→
    73→- Deterministic execution.
    74→- Reproducible results.
    75→- Explainable scheduling decisions.
    76→- Modular architecture.
    77→- Platform-independent local execution.
    78→- Extensibility for real Ray and Kubernetes integration.
    79→- Secure and maintainable code.
    80→- Clear separation between CP‑1 baseline and CP‑2 controller.
    81→
    82→#### Initial constraints identified
    83→
    84→- No dedicated GPU is required for the CP‑1 simulator.
    85→- A real Ray/Kubernetes cluster may not be available during early development.
    86→- Placement behaviour must be modelled consistently with Ray semantics.
    87→- CP‑1 must not modify scheduling decisions through the observer.
    88→- Every experiment must terminate within a fixed time.
    89→- CP‑1 results must remain unchanged when the CP‑2 scheduler is later introduced.
    90→
    91→#### Candidate solutions compared
    92→
    93→| Candidate | Description | Main limitation |
    94→|---|---|---|
    95→| Direct Ray modification | Modify Ray’s internal scheduler | High complexity and difficult to maintain |
    96→| External admission controller | Intercept requests before Ray scheduling | Requires real Ray integration early |
    97→| Deterministic simulator | Reproduce nested-resource behaviour locally | Does not directly prove production Ray performance |
    98→| Hybrid approach | Deterministic simulator plus optional Ray adapter | Requires careful separation of simulated and live evidence |
    99→
   100→#### Design decision
   101→
   102→The team selected the **hybrid approach**:
   103→
   104→- A dependency-light deterministic simulator for CP‑1.
   105→- FIFO and greedy baseline policies.
   106→- An independent wait-for observer.
   107→- A later real-Ray adapter.
   108→- CP‑2 extension for deadlock-aware admission, recovery and dashboard integration.
   109→
   110→This approach was selected because it provides reproducible evidence on a laptop while keeping the architecture extensible to Ray and Kubernetes.
   111→
   112→### Initial CP‑1 architecture
   113→
   114→```mermaid
   115→flowchart LR
   116→    A["Workload JSON"] --> B["Schema validator"]

   119→    D --> E["Bounded simulator"]

   123→
   124→The important design decision is that the observer only detects and reports cycles. It does not change FIFO or greedy scheduling decisions. This keeps the CP‑1 baseline fair and prevents accidental CP‑2 behaviour from appearing in the baseline results.
